# Short clips

User requirement (2026-10-07): once a video is done, the app also makes short-format clips from it, taking inspiration from BridgeClip (github.com/bridge-mind/bridgeclip, MIT). This document records what was adopted, what was adapted and what remains.

## What BridgeClip does, in brief

BridgeClip turns long videos into captioned short clips. It transcribes the whole source, asks a planning model for moments with explicit setup and payoff, then has a separate judge check each candidate for a clear opening, a complete ending, self-contained clarity and sponsorship, with fixed thresholds and no minimum clip count. It reframes to 9:16 by following faces and fitting screen content into panels, burns in captions in several styles, offers 1× to 2× speed with pitch kept, and keeps every export in a library. Its documents were read as research only; no code was copied. It was studied again on 2026-11-02 for the engine described in [57](57_CLIPS_ENGINE_PLAN.md); still no code, text or tests were copied.

## What this app does now

Short clips live in the Export step, after the main export, because they are made from the finished edit rather than from the raw source.

- **Find clips** reads the transcript of the current draft (output time, with text corrections, partly cut words dropped) and proposes up to six non-overlapping moments of 15 to 60 seconds (`packages/domain/src/short-clips.ts`). Each starts at a sentence and ends at the end of a sentence (or a pause of 1.2 s). Scores favour a new thought at the start (clips start only where a sentence starts; openings such as "And", "But", "That", "It" that usually depend on earlier context are penalised, and "So" or "Anyway" only slightly), an opening question, a complete final sentence, 25 to 45 seconds, and steady speech with few long silences or fillers. Only candidates scoring at least 0.55 are offered and there is no minimum count, so a draft without a good moment gets none. Cards show the title (the first sentence), time range, the reasons that applied and an excerpt. These are labelled as found from the transcript; they are local rules, not an AI judgment.
- **Pick with Claude** (2026-10-25) lets Claude choose instead. It starts a read-only Claude run with a clip-picking playbook (`apps/desktop/src/shorts-pick-playbook.ts`): it reads `edit.md` (the edit as spoken, with edit times), may look at rendered pictures, and ends in one call of the Claude-only tool `shorts.propose` with up to six clips in rank order, each with a title and one to four reasons. Main allows only reading, `media.get_frames` and `shorts.propose` during that run; any edit is refused. The app checks every proposal itself with the same rules the local finder keeps (`proposeShortCandidates`): each edge snaps to the nearest sentence edge within 3 s (a clip starts where a sentence starts and ends where one ends), the length must be 15 to 60 s, clips may not overlap, a title is required, and the excerpt comes from the draft's own words, never from the model. The answer lists what was refused and why so Claude can correct and call again; the accepted clips replace the panel's earlier set and are marked "Claude's pick". It needs a transcript and a signed-in Claude; it does not change the edit and exports nothing.
- **Preview** plays the clip in the main player and stops at its end. **Discard** hides a candidate. Candidates are stored per project with the draft head they were found on; after an edit they are marked as out of date until found again.
- **Export clip** asks for a file name and renders an MP4 through the same verified pipeline as the main export: the clip's output range is mapped through the clip map to source ranges, frames are reframed in one composition stage and encoded, and the file is checked for every frame before it appears. Formats: vertical 9:16 (1080×1920), square 1:1 (1080×1080) and landscape 16:9 (1920×1080). Framing: **Fit with blurred background** keeps the whole picture over a blurred, darkened fill of itself, which suits screen recordings; **Fill the frame** crops to the format at a chosen position. Captions are burned in by default using the project's caption style with shorter lines (26 characters for vertical and square) and, for vertical, placed 20% up from the bottom, clear of the controls social apps draw there.
- **Show title** puts the clip's own title over its first three seconds (a dark pill with white text, fading in and out, near the top of the picture), drawn as a graphic before the reframing like the draft's own graphics. Its box is kept inside the part of the frame the format shows: for **Fill** it stays within the cropped slice at the chosen position.
- **Speed** plays the clip at normal speed or 2×, with the voice's pitch kept (the render supports whole-number speeds). It needs every part of the clip to allow it: parts already at 3× or 8× cannot go faster and the export says so. Captions, zooms and transitions move with the speed; a title and the draft's own graphics keep the time they are on screen.
- **Export all** asks for one folder and exports every clip in it, one after another, named for their titles (a repeated name gets a number), with one progress line (clip 3 of 6) and one Cancel. A failure stops the run; clips already saved stay saved and listed.
- **Saved clips** lists every clip exported from the project, newest first, by file name, format, length and speed (never the folder path): **Show in folder** opens the system file manager at the file, and **Remove from list** forgets the entry without touching the file. A clip whose file is gone says so. The list survives finding clips again and is removed with the project.

## Differences from BridgeClip, and why

- The planner is Claude through a guarded tool, with the local rules as the fallback; there is no separate judge model. Claude does not quote transcript lines in its proposal: the app derives the excerpt from the draft, so a suggestion is checkable at a glance. Codex and the API providers are not offered `shorts.propose` (it needs the project workspace that only Claude has).
- Face following (2026-11-02, Rust engine, [57](57_CLIPS_ENGINE_PLAN.md)). **Fill the frame** follows the speaker's face when the engine is present and the pinned face model could be fetched: faces are detected on two frames per second, a webcam bubble in a corner is ignored, and a calm camera path (dead zone, speed limit, held through short gaps) moves the crop. With no usable face the crop stays at the chosen position, as before. **Fit** is unchanged, because the whole screen is safer for screen recordings. Webcam-aware layouts come later with camera layouts (P8-02).
- Speed is 1× or 2×, not a free 1× to 2× slider; there are no chapters or posting, and a title is the clip's own title (it cannot be edited separately).
- The saved-clips list is a list of files, not a media library: it holds the last 100 and stores no thumbnails.

## Engine (2026-11-02)

Clips are rendered by the Rust engine (`engine/`, [58](58_RUST_ENGINE_PLAN.md)) when it is packaged, in one FFmpeg pass with an exact-frame check of the result, one retry on a safer path (software encoder, fixed crop) and cleanup of partial files. The TypeScript pipeline above stays as the fallback for audio cleanup, a missing or failing engine, and as the parity reference (`tests/media/short-engine.test.ts`). `reframeFilter` now crops before scaling for **Fill** and blurs the **Fit** backdrop at a quarter of the size, which speeds both paths.
